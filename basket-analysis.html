Module 03

What is Market Basket Analysis?

Market Basket Analysis studies millions of shopping carts to answer one simple question: "When a customer buys product A, what else do they usually buy?" Supermarkets used it to place bread near butter; e-commerce companies use it to power "Frequently Bought Together" and recommendation engines. It works by mining association rules from transaction data using algorithms like Apriori.

The three key metrics

How a rule is measured

Every rule like "Milk → Bread" is scored on three numbers before it's trusted.

Support

How often the pair appears together in all baskets. A support of 12% means 12 out of every 100 orders contain both items.

Support = P(A ∩ B)

Confidence

Of the baskets that contain A, how many also contain B. A confidence of 68% means milk buyers pick up bread 68% of the time.

Confidence = P(B | A)

Lift

How much more likely B is bought because of A, versus by chance. Lift above 1 means a genuine relationship, not coincidence.

Lift = Confidence ÷ P(B)
